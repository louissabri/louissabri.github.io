Robotic Rammed Earth

3D printed formworks for a sustainable rammed earth structure.

Project Overview

This project investigates the potential of combining traditional rammed earth construction techniques with advanced digital fabrication methods. By designing and 3D printing custom formworks for rammed earth structures, I aimed to create a hybrid construction process that leverages the ecological benefits of earth materials with the precision and complexity enabled by computational design.

Technologies Used

  • Rhino 3D and Grasshopper (Parametric Design)
  • Robot control programming with KUKA|prc
  • Large-scale 3D printing with biodegradable materials
  • Material research and earth mixture optimization

Research & Development

The project began with extensive research into traditional rammed earth construction techniques and their material properties. I then explored how digital design tools could enhance these techniques while maintaining their sustainability benefits.

Through computational design, I developed parametric formwork systems that could create complex geometries not possible with traditional formwork. These systems were optimized for robotic fabrication and designed to be biodegradable after use.

Fabrication Process

The formworks were 3D printed using a KUKA robotic arm equipped with a custom extrusion system. This allowed for precise control over the geometry and enabled the creation of complex patterns that would influence the final appearance of the rammed earth wall.

Once the formworks were printed, locally-sourced earth materials were carefully mixed and compressed into the forms using both traditional and mechanized ramming techniques. After curing, the biodegradable formworks were removed, revealing the final structure with its designed textures and patterns.

Outcomes & Impact

The resulting structure demonstrates how traditional building methods can be enhanced with digital fabrication technologies to create more sustainable and visually distinctive architecture. The project achieved a 75% reduction in carbon footprint compared to conventional concrete construction methods while maintaining structural integrity.

This research has been presented at several sustainable architecture conferences and has influenced ongoing discussions about how digital technologies can support more ecological building practices.

Future Directions

Building on this project, I am now exploring how the same principles can be applied to larger architectural elements and potentially entire building systems. I am also investigating new biodegradable materials for the formworks that can further reduce the environmental impact of the construction process.
